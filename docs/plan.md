## P1 — companion

### P1-SCROLL — he still lags behind the page
> *"The sparrow still glitches when you scroll, he should stay where he is on
> the page, not the screen. The updates take too long for the scroll and he
> moves and has to readjust."*

**Reported three times. Still open. This is the biggest defect on the page.**

**The cause is not staleness, it is compositing order.** Browsers scroll on the
compositor thread without waiting for the main thread. A rAF callback reading
`window.scrollY` gets the last *committed* offset, which during a fling is
behind what the compositor has already painted. The bird is therefore drawn at a
position derived from an older scroll offset, every frame, and corrects on the
next one. No amount of reading scroll "more freshly" fixes it, because the main
thread is structurally behind. The only fixes take JS out of the scroll path.

**Do not attempt this in the same pass as anything else, and take a copy first.**
`components/v2/` is untracked (see P0-GIT). `Companion.tsx` is ~4,950 lines.

#### The constraints, measured, so the next attempt does not rediscover them

- `.v2-bird-layer` is `position: fixed; inset: 0` (v2.css:687).
- The canvas is **viewport-sized**: `W = window.innerWidth`, `H = window.innerHeight`
  (Companion.tsx:1192). A full-document canvas is NOT an option — at 13,000px
  tall and DPR 2 that is ~66M pixels, past what browsers will allocate.
- **The chat window is drawn on the same canvas** and positioned in VIEWPORT
  coordinates, clamped against `W`/`H` (Companion.tsx:1789-1801). Any change to
  the canvas box has to deal with this. The chat's input and log are already
  DOM; it is the frame and caret that are canvas.
- The bird's own coordinates are already document-space (`bird.x`, `bird.y`),
  and perches are harvested in document space. **The maths is already right.**
  Only the paint path is wrong.

#### Preferred fix: take JS out of the scroll path

Split the single fixed canvas into two elements:

1. **A sprite canvas in document flow.** A container in normal flow, and inside
   it a small canvas (roughly 240x240 CSS px — sprite plus prop overhang plus
   the balloon's reach) positioned with
   `transform: translate3d(bird.x px, bird.y px, 0)` in DOCUMENT coordinates.
   The compositor then scrolls it with the page for free and the draw call never
   reads `scrollY` at all. Draw the sprite at a fixed offset within it.
2. **Keep the fixed viewport canvas for the chat frame only**, which is
   genuinely viewport-anchored and has no lag problem because it does not move
   while you scroll.

`translate3d` rather than `left`/`top` so it stays on the compositor. The
transform only changes when he moves under his own power, not when the page
scrolls.

#### Alternative: virtual scroll

Adopt Lenis and have it own the tick that the companion, the ink field and the
worlds all read from (`autoRaf: false`, drive `lenis.raf()` from one ticker,
`lagSmoothing(0)`). This removes the race by making scroll position a JS value
everyone reads on the same tick. It also buys the weighted smooth scroll that
By-Kin was specifically credited for — see [research.md](research.md) section 4.
It is a bigger decision than a bird fix, because it takes over scrolling for the
whole site, and it deserves its own A/B.

#### What can and cannot be verified

Not verifiable in this environment at all: the smoothness, which is the entire
point. Scrolling is a no-op in the Browser pane (see spec section 8).

Verifiable: that he still lands on the right perches (compare
`__bird.perches()` before and after), that document coordinates are unchanged,
that nothing throws, that the sprite still composites correctly, and that the
chat still opens and closes. Do all of those, and then hand it to Jack to say
whether it actually feels fixed.

## P1 — projects

### P1-SCENES — DONE (2026-08-25)
*"On the projects, the scenes don't really show anything, they're very
obscured. Just make them normal 3D objects."*

Not one problem. **Three separate bugs, each of which alone would have wrecked
the plate**, plus the collage:

`[x]` **The glyph atlas painted opaque black tiles.** It filled every cell with
`#000` and drew a white glyph on top, so each cell blitted as a solid black
square with a speck in it. On a paper page that is a dark rectangle where a
drawing should be. This was the dominant cause and it MASKED the other two.
Atlas is transparent now, glyphs painted white and tinted at blit time with a
single `source-in` pass, which also makes the plate follow the live section
palette without rebuilding the atlas. Ink coverage went 30.8% to 4.8%.

`[x]` **The glyph ramp was not inverted.** `gi = floor(v * RAMP.length)` maps a
BRIGHT face to the densest character. That is correct on a black terminal and
backwards on paper, where a lit face has to leave the paper showing. Inverted,
with the background rejected before the inversion rather than after.

`[x]` **`LIGHT` was shadowed.** A leftover local `const LIGHT = [0.42, 0.72,
0.55]` sat inside the render effect, pointing away from the camera. Once the
normals were flipped to face the viewer it made every dot product negative, so
every face clamped to lambert 0 and shaded to exactly `tone * 0.14`. Measured:
shade range was 0.095-0.12 across all seven faces; it is 0.553-0.67 now.
Removed, with a comment saying not to reintroduce it.

`[x]` And the shading was a **winding test, not a normal** — the sign of the
projected cross product, giving each face one of two values. Replaced with a
Newell normal over the view-space polygon and a real lambert term. Newell
rather than a three-vertex cross product because several solids have
non-planar quads that flip at random.

`[x]` **One object per plate, not three.** Three solids at 1.0 / 0.62 / 0.52
occlude each other into one silhouette at 87x31 cells. The first collage item
is the object; the rest of the list stays in `content.ts` as intent.

`[ ]` Open, and a taste call: `paperStack` is a weak lead object for
Recensorium. Seven parallel panels all catch the light the same way, so the
shade range is narrow by construction. Objects with faces at varied angles
(`trophy`, `folder`, `monitor`) read far better through this pipeline.

`[ ]` InkWash is still the other intended target.

### P2-FALLBACK — no fallback exists for the WebGL worlds
`[ ]` The judged round-up is blunt that ambition without a fallback loses the
third of the audience whose hardware cannot run it. Three of our eight worlds
own a GL context and there is no degraded path. Needs one.

### P2-TRANSITIONS — our section changes are cuts
`[ ]` The clearest finding of the whole pass: cheap sites cut, award-winners
move, and transition continuity is what separates the tiers. Our section reveal
is a single CSS class flip, which is the cut pattern. This is the same problem
as **P0-PALETTE** — six colour schemes with six abrupt changes would be six
cuts. The crossfade must carry the type colours, not just the canvas.

### P2-VOCABULARY — we may have too many effects
`[ ]` Award-winning sites are reported to use a *small* vocabulary of effects
applied with taste. We have eight worlds, a companion, an ASCII wall, a neural
playground, polaroids, a 3D reel, a route map and a skills cloud. Worth asking
Jack which of these are the vocabulary and which are noise. Not a code task — a
conversation, and probably the second-most valuable one after P0-STORY.

### P2-KINETIC — plate titles are static
`[ ]` Kinetic typography on a timeline is a recurring winner. We have one
masked-and-lifted hero and nothing after it. A restrained treatment on the plate
titles is on-brand for an engineering journal and currently absent.

### P2-ASCII-GPU — procedural glyphs in GLSL
`[ ]` **Option, not a task.** Glyphs can be generated as functions over a 5x7
grid in GLSL rather than blitted from an atlas — no texture, character set
becomes code. Our canvas-2D atlas is already fast and correct; this only matters
if we want much higher cell density or per-cell effects. Filed so it is not
rediscovered.
