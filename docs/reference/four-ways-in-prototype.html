What you are looking at

A · Glyph Engine

A displaced sphere is raymarched live, but at one ray per character cell rather than per pixel, so a full screen costs about 16,000 rays instead of two million. The lit result picks a glyph from a ramp. Your cursor is the light. This is the direction that is most yours: the site is literally rendered by a pipeline, the way your Python rasterizer was.

B · Latent Field

262,144 particles simulated on the GPU in ping-pong float textures, advected through a curl-noise field, pulled toward a target shape sampled from rendered type. Cursor injects repulsion. Reads as latent space and as fluid at the same time.

C · Field Notes

Cold technical paper rather than warm cream. Contour lines are real marching squares over a moving noise surface, drawn at 30fps on a 2D canvas. Costs almost nothing, works everywhere, and lets typography carry the whole page.

D · Sumi

A genuine fluid solver: advection, divergence, twenty Jacobi pressure iterations, gradient subtraction, all at 60fps on a small grid. The dye it carries is rendered as watercolour rather than smoke, with edge darkening so pigment pools where it stops, plus procedural washi fibre. Move to lay sumi black, hold to press vermilion. The seal is the only fixed mark on the page.

Deciding

These are poles, not finished pages. My instinct: D is the ground and A is the instrument — ink-wash paper and ma for the calm, the glyph renderer for the technical set pieces, vermilion as the single accent throughout. B's particle field then belongs to one section rather than the whole site. Tell me where you want to stand.
